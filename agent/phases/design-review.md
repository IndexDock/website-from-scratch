# Design review

## Goal

Present genuinely distinct homepage concepts using the same approved facts and content.

## Actions

Apply `agent/contracts/orchestration.md`, `agent/contracts/state.md`, `agent/contracts/content-integrity.md`, `agent/rubrics/design-craft.md`, and `agent/rubrics/design-review.md`.

Every concept must clear the distinctiveness floor in `agent/rubrics/design-craft.md`: a named palette, a deliberate type pairing and scale, a stated layout concept, and one signature element, none of them arrived at by default. Where the packet is silent, report the gap and design around it; never invent subject matter or copy to give a layout something to hold.

When subagents are supported, automatically give the completed design packet to one design worker, then give the resulting artifacts to an independent read-only design auditor. Otherwise perform the same bounded work in the main task, meeting the same craft standard — the standard belongs to the work, not to the worker. Create three concepts by default, two when strong references or branding narrow the space, or one main concept plus a conservative alternative under strict constraints. Fewer than three requires the narrowing constraint to actually exist and to be named in `project/DECISIONS.md` before the concepts are built. A stated colour preference is not a narrowing constraint; supplied branding, a required reference, or an explicit learner restriction is. Neither the design packet nor a worker may relax this — where a downstream document disagrees with this rule, this rule wins. The main orchestrator verifies the result and presents it to the learner, who may approve, revise, combine one base with up to two named elements, or reject all.

Show the concepts on the live site, not on a local server: save them under `public/design-review/`, set `implementation.siteMode` to `design-review`, push, and let promotion deploy them. Give the learner the exact `workers.dev` review URLs. A cloud session has no browser the learner can reach, and the same URLs work identically for a learner running the agent locally. Review routes stay `noindex` and are removed before publication.

Handing those URLs over is the moment the learner has something of their own to do again, so set `workflow.courseCheckpointId` to `choose-design` when the concepts are ready and the review URLs go out. `agent/course-checkpoints.json` lists that identifier under the title "Choose the design". This is the only place it is recorded: `agent/phases/design-discovery.md` parks the checkpoint at `share-references` from the moment the reference screenshots are requested and leaves it there deliberately, so a phase that never records `choose-design` leaves the learner's visible course position stuck on a step they finished long ago.

In the same message, say one thing about cost: each round of changes means rebuilding the concept and waiting for it to deploy again, so everything they dislike should come in one go rather than a piece at a time. It is the same economy that makes `agent/contracts/assets.md` ask for all the reference screenshots in a single request. Saying it once, at handover, is what makes the round cap below read as arithmetic rather than as a refusal.

Revision is where most of the work of this phase actually happens, so it has a procedure of its own. The four responses above stay exactly four: what follows is what "revise" means, not a fifth thing the learner may pick. A learner who wants changes has chosen revise, and the rest of this section is the shape that choice takes.

Ask them to settle on one concept before hearing a single complaint, then improve that one. Repairing all three multiplies every fix across two concepts that are about to be discarded, which is work nobody will ever see. Picking first also sharpens the complaints themselves: an objection to the concept somebody has decided to live with is a more useful thing than an objection to one of three options still on the table. A learner who wants the type from one and the spacing from another has not made a revision request at all; that is the combine response, and it is answered by naming a base and up to two elements, as above.

Complaints are far easier to make in pictures than in words, so invite the learner to attach an image of what they mean directly in the conversation. This is deliberately the opposite of the rule for reference screenshots in `agent/contracts/assets.md`, and the asymmetry is worth understanding rather than reading as an inconsistency. A reference screenshot is upstream input: the design worker and the visual auditor have to see it, and it has to outlive the session that collected it, so it must reach the repository. A complaint screenshot is transient feedback about the learner's own site, it is acted on within minutes, and the only party who needs to see it is the orchestrator already holding the conversation.

The practical cost settles it. Every learner upload commits to `main`, which obliges the `origin/main` merge in `agent/contracts/git-delivery.md` before anything else is touched. Three rounds of complaints delivered that way is three more chances to fail promotion on work that was correct, bought in exchange for nothing at all.

Sort every complaint into exactly one of three buckets, and put each item in one bucket only:

- A **defect** is a visual bug: content overlapping, a layout broken at some width, text that cannot be read against what sits behind it. Fix it now, and do not count it against the round cap. A defect is not the learner being particular, and charging them a round for reporting one charges them for the agent's own mistake.
- A **direction** problem is the design genuinely not suiting the business: the wrong register, the wrong weight, a mood that misreads the trade. Fix it now, and count it against the cap. This is the category the cap exists for.
- A **refinement** is taste-level polish: a heading a little larger, a section a little roomier, a colour a shade off. Record it in the Direction section of `project/DESIGN-DIRECTION.md` for the implementation phase to act on, rather than fixing it now.

Do the sorting out loud, item by item, and let the learner object before you act on it. Silent sorting is the mechanism by which "we will handle that later" becomes a way of never handling it, and the learner is the only person in a position to say that what was filed as a refinement is in fact a defect. They cannot say it if the filing happens somewhere they cannot hear it.

The refinement bucket is not a new rule. It is the rule the Exit section below already states — that approval confirms direction and composition rather than pixel-perfect implementation, and that small implementation-stage refinements remain in implementation or quality review — now carrying a name and, more usefully, a place to be written down. Direction is the rewritable half of the file in `agent/phases/design-discovery.md`, so a refinement written there travels into the implementation packet that `agent/phases/implementation.md` builds from the approved design, where a refinement agreed only in conversation is one that quietly does not happen. Never put one in the interview section instead: that half is append-only and records what the learner told us, not what is to be built.

Repair defects yourself in the main task, and send genuine direction changes to the design worker. The dividing line is the size of the judgement rather than the size of the edit. A worker cannot see an image attached in the conversation — the same limitation `agent/contracts/assets.md` describes for a pasted reference — so a repair briefed from one is translated into words before it reaches a worker whichever route it takes, and the worker gains nothing from the picture. Against that, a fresh worker starts cold and, as `agent/subagents.md` requires, reads the craft rubric and its design method file in full before touching anything. That is the right price for reconsidering a palette, a type pairing, or a layout concept, which is craft work the rubric governs and the worker exists to do. It is the wrong price for closing a gap of a few pixels.

Three rounds is the cap. Defects do not consume a round, so a learner who has found a real bug has spent nothing. At the cap, name what is happening rather than opening a fourth round in silence, and which of two things is happening is decided by what is left on the list.

If only refinements remain, say so plainly, confirm they are recorded in the Direction section, and ask for approval on that basis. That is the honest position rather than a way of closing the phase: the direction is settled, and what is left is implementation work that already has somewhere to go.

If a third round is still producing direction problems, the concept is not the thing that is failing. Something the interview did not surface is, and a fourth repair round will not find it, because a repair round tests a concept rather than a brief. Reopen the design conversation instead, following `agent/phases/design-discovery.md`, with the rendered concept in hand as evidence. That is a better starting point than the first interview had: the learner is reacting to a real page built for their own business rather than to somebody else's website or to an adjective. Append what they say now to the interview section as a dated correction beneath the original entry, as that module requires, rather than editing over what they said before — that a stated preference did not survive contact with a rendered page is exactly the finding the next direction should be built on.

## Exit

Save review artifacts under `public/design-review/`, record the selected concept in `project/DESIGN-DIRECTION.md`, and set `approvals.design` only after explicit approval. Approval confirms the design direction and composition, not pixel-perfect implementation. Small implementation-stage refinements remain in implementation or quality review; a material direction change reopens design approval. Do not start implementation without approval.
