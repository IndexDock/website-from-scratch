# Design review rubric

Evaluate business fit, hierarchy, legibility, distinctness, responsive intent, factual integrity, asset realism, navigation behavior, motion and reduced-motion intent, and whether the primary action remains clear. Concepts must differ in several meaningful design dimensions, not merely colour. Design approval selects a direction; implementation and quality review still own final responsive polish.

Also evaluate against `agent/rubrics/design-craft.md`: whether the palette, type pairing and scale, layout concept, and signature element are stated and specific to this business, whether the work landed on one of the named defaults, whether structural devices encode real content — an eyebrow over most or every section heading, restating the heading rather than a category, a step in a real sequence, or an actual navigation target, fails this — and whether font delivery and motion respect the gate in `agent/contracts/quality.md`. Generation and review share one bar.

## Defect checklist for concepts

Craft and correctness are different questions, and a concept can clear every standard above while still being broken. Before any concept is shown to the learner, check it for these:

- content overlapping other content, or text colliding with an image or an edge;
- a layout that breaks or overflows at any width in the matrix in `agent/contracts/quality.md`;
- text that cannot be read against what sits behind it;
- an element that renders empty, collapsed, or off-screen;
- a display heading still sitting at body or default line-height when it is not overflowing, or a headline whose lines are ragged — one long line and a stray word — rather than balanced to near-equal length or staggered on purpose. `agent/contracts/typography.md` is the rule and `npm run validate:typography` catches the mechanical half, but whether the shape reads as deliberate at each width in the matrix is the auditor's to judge;
- three or more short, independent, non-sequential facts — hours, service areas, contact channels — glued into one line with `·`, `•`, `|`, or a slash, or dumped into a bare default-marker list, instead of given individual boundaries such as a row of chips, cards, a grid, or a table. `agent/rubrics/design-craft.md`'s "Parallel facts get counted, not run together" is the rule and `npm run validate:icons` catches a literal separator character mechanically, but whether the chosen device fits what the facts are, and whether a bare list of three or more of them still reads as one undifferentiated block, is the auditor's to judge;
- a group of peer buttons — same role, same weight, presented as equal choices — coming out ragged, each sized to its own label, or left unbounded so a longer label later pushes one sibling wider than the rest or wraps to several lines while its siblings stay on one. `agent/rubrics/design-craft.md`'s "Buttons that are peers share one width" is the rule: the group should share one width, centred in its container, with an overflowing label truncated to an ellipsis rather than reflowing the button or stretching it. There is no mechanical check for this — it is a rendered-layout fact, not a literal character — so whether the shared width and the truncation actually hold at each width in the matrix is the auditor's alone to judge;
- a stated tier breached — an observation the learner tagged "just the feel" reproduced closely enough to be a copy of the reference.

This checklist lives with the auditor rather than with the learner because the learner is the last line, not the first. They were asked to choose between three designs; a learner who opens the preview link and has to report that two headings sit on top of each other has been handed a different job, and one they did not agree to. Worse, the confidence is spent before the real question is reached — every judgement they make about the direction afterwards is made through the impression that nobody looked at the page before sending it.

Part of the ground is covered automatically. `tests/design-review.spec.ts` catches horizontal overflow at each width, serious and critical automated accessibility violations including colour contrast, and a page that failed to render at all. It is a real check and not a formality, but it runs only in continuous integration and only on the defects a machine can judge, so it is a floor under this checklist rather than a replacement for it. The rest are the auditor's, and the tier breach above all: no automated check can see one, because nothing in the rendered page records which reference the concept was built from or what the learner said about it.
